# NAMI trailer — three storylines

One thing the film is about: **anime from `ani-cli`, in a real window instead of a terminal.**
One viewer, one real task: find *Frieren: Beyond Journey's End* and play episode 3. That title is the
piece of data that travels from the hook to the payoff.

Facts on screen come from the shipped app: the home heading "What are you watching?", the search field
with its `Ctrl K` chip, "Continue watching", "Trending now", the episode list, and the glass player
with its scrub bar. NAMI hosts nothing and needs `ani-cli` and `yt-dlp` installed.

Assumptions unless you say otherwise: 45–55 s, 1920×1080 at 30 fps, no voiceover, synthesized music and
sound only (nothing paid).

---

## 1. A night in one take — recommended

**Device:** a moment in someone's day. **Form:** one continuous take, no cuts.

The camera starts outside the window in the dark and pushes in without ever cutting. The search field
swallows the title, the results grid opens out of it, the episode list unfolds from the poster, and the
player's glass controls rise out of the video. It feels like one calm hand moving through one room.

**First two seconds, exactly:** frame 0 is the NAMI window already open on "What are you watching?",
caret blinking in the search field. At 0.6 s one real keystroke lands and the `F` appears.

**Beats:** hook (window, caret, first key) → tension (a terminal line `ani-cli frieren` waits for Enter,
then prints faster than anyone can read) → reveal (a glass panel rises and wipes the text; NAMI named)
→ proof (search → results → episode list → play, four verbs) → payoff (the player: 1080p, scrub moving,
the episode actually playing) → claim → end card.

**Look:** ground `#06090a`; Bricolage Grotesque for display; the one material is the app's own dark
glass with a 1 px inner highlight. Type sits inside the app, never over a dimmed screenshot.

**Sound:** synthesized. A low sub pulse under the terminal, one glass click per real action, a rising
pad as the player opens.

**Why it is first:** it is the only one where the real UI performs the actions, so the proof is the
product working rather than a picture of it. The terminal is a 6 s beat, not the subject, so NAMI is
not sold against the tool it drives.

---

## 2. Two rooms

**Device:** life before and after. **Form:** split screen to one.

The first half is a split: left, a terminal scrolling `ani-cli` output; right, the same titles as
posters in NAMI. Every line that prints on the left has its calm twin appear on the right at the same
instant. At about 40% the left pane collapses and the app takes the whole frame. It feels like a
comparison you can watch resolve.

**First two seconds, exactly:** the split already exists. The terminal prints its first line and the
matching poster fades in on the right on the same frame.

**Beats:** hook (the split, first twin pair) → tension (the left pane fills with text and slows)
→ reveal (the right pane stops waiting) → proof (search, posters, episode list on the right only)
→ payoff (the left pane collapses; the player fills the frame) → claim → end card.

**Look:** one frame, two palettes. Terminal green-black with mono on the left, NAMI `#06090a` glass on
the right; `#ff8a66` appears once, on the frame the left pane dies.

**Sound:** two synthesized textures panned hard left and right that merge into one when the panes merge.

---

## 3. Pick a title, any title

**Device:** type carries the argument. **Form:** cut on the beat.

Giant kinetic type on NAMI's ground, hard cuts on a synthesized beat, and every claim gets its proof as
a real UI fragment that punches in and leaves: "Search" → the real search field; "Trending" → the real
grid; "Play" → the real player. It feels like a drum solo where every hit shows a real screen.

**First two seconds, exactly:** the word `TERMINAL` in 200 px type slams in and cracks apart on the
first beat into the four letters of NAMI.

**Beats:** hook (`TERMINAL` cracks into NAMI) → tension (type stacks into a wall of commands)
→ reveal (the wall is swept away and the real search field lands) → proof (three beats, each one word
plus one real screen) → payoff (the player, on the loudest hit) → claim → end card.

**Look:** `#06090a` ground, Bricolage at display weight filling 60–80% of the frame, `#ff8a66` rationed
to one word per beat; the treatment is a 1-frame cut with a 3 px white flash and no glow.

**Sound:** a synthesized drum pattern; every cut lands on a hit, and the film stops on the last one.
